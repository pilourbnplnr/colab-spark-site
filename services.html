Four ways to move the work forward

It has never been more important to work hand in hand to tackle today's problems — and it has never been as hard. Below is what that looks like in practice, with real examples from client engagements and prior roles.

Research + Policy

Diagnose the landscape
Client: Alta Housing

Funding Mechanisms for Affordable Housing Production & Preservation

LIHTC used to be the answer. It isn't anymore — not on its own, not with construction costs where they are and competition for credits where it's at. So when Alta Housing needed to know what else was actually out there, I went and found it.

Across a 17-week research engagement, I mapped alternative and supplementary funding sources across three priorities that don't usually get analyzed together: acquisition and rehab (including NOAH preservation), development on faith-owned land, and programs that prioritize low- and moderate-resourced neighborhoods under CalEnviroScreen 4.0. That meant digging into everything from HUD Section 221(d)(3) to CalHFA's Mixed-Income Program, running a dozen-plus interviews with developers, funders, and land trusts, and turning it into a comparative funding matrix Alta's team can actually use to make decisions.

Status: Current client. Findings confidential until Alta determines public release.

Deputy Director, Silicon Valley at Home (SV@Home)

Best Practices for Local Affordable Housing Policy

Good housing policy rarely fails for lack of ideas — it fails because nobody's translated the idea into language a city council or planning department can actually adopt.

As Deputy Director, I oversaw research into affordable housing policy tools — inclusionary zoning, impact fees, land use planning — and turned that research into best-practice recommendations SV@Home then used to advocate directly, both for standalone policies and for inclusion in large-scale master and specific plans, across jurisdictions throughout Santa Clara County.

Status: Past role (SV@Home, 2015–2018).

Engagement + Coalition Building

Build the relationships
Client: Santa Clara Valley Transportation Authority (VTA)

Strategic & Philanthropic Partnerships for Transit-Oriented Community Development

Community development, affordable housing, public art, and public transit rarely get funded — or planned — by the same people. That's the gap this work sits in.

I built strategic and philanthropic partnership profiles connecting VTA's Transit Oriented Communities priorities to the funders, developers, and civic partners positioned to support them — spanning housing, arts, and transit in a single, coordinated strategy rather than four disconnected asks.

Status: Current client.

Coalition Lead, while at SV@Home

North Bayshore Specific Plan

Some of the hardest work in planning isn't the plan itself — it's getting a room full of people with competing interests to actually agree on one.

I led multi-sector coalition building across employers, housing advocates, transportation planners, and local government, translating competing priorities into a plan that could actually move — not just look good on paper.

Status: Past project (SV@Home).

Capacity Building

Strengthen the people who carry it forward
Program Director, ChangeLab Solutions — in partnership with NALEO

Public Policy Curriculum for Social Determinants of Health

Elected officials can't act on what they don't understand — and health equity policy is exactly the kind of thing most local electeds were never trained on.

As Program Director, I developed a yearlong curriculum for the National Association of Latino Elected and Appointed Officials (NALEO) teaching how public policy levers — land use, housing, transportation — actually move the social determinants of health. It was deployed annually over multiple years, building a sustained pipeline of electeds who understood the connection between policy and health outcomes in their own districts.

Status: Past role (ChangeLab Solutions, 2009–2013).

Co-Chair, while at SVCF

Great Communities Collaborative

Some coalitions ask partners to show up. This one gave them the resources to actually build the muscle to keep showing up.

As Co-Chair, I helped steward a multi-funder collaborative that seeded capacity directly into public, private, and nonprofit partners — funding organizations to grow their own ability to advocate for shared goals around housing, economic development, and transit.

Status: Past role (2023–2025), Co-Chair.

Strategy + Implementation

Get it done
Community Development Manager, Google

Social Infrastructure Plan

Most strategy documents get written, presented, praised, and shelved. This one became the operating template for a 350-acre real-estate portfolio.

I authored Google's first Social Infrastructure Plan — a five-year framework spanning climate justice, affordable housing, small business, and community development — and then ran it: managing a $3M annual budget, spearheading a proposed $70M revolving loan fund to jumpstart 2,500+ affordable homes, and building a $1M small-business program that stabilized ten BIPOC-owned food businesses.

Status: Past role (Google, 2019–2023).

VP, Community Action, Initiatives & Policy, Silicon Valley Community Foundation (SVCF)

Integrated Capital Fund

Grantmaking moves money once. A fund is designed to move it again and again — a different discipline, and a different kind of implementation.

I helped launch and then managed SVCF's Integrated Capital Fund, a social-impact investment pilot that directed $12M into regional CDFIs to create and preserve affordable housing and seed small businesses in underserved communities.

Status: Past role (SVCF, 2023–2025).
